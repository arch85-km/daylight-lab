### 1.2 Sky integrals

The discretised sky must reproduce the hemisphere integrals `∫L·cosθ·dω`:
2.44954·L_z for CIE Standard General Sky type 1, the overcast sky both
engines use, and `π·L` for a uniform sky. Type 1 has no elementary closed
form, so its value is numerical. It is close to — but not — the 7π/9 =
2.44346 of the Moon & Spencer sky, and comparing against that constant is
what previously reported a 0.22% error here that was mostly definitional.

| Subdivision | Patches | Σω vs 2π | Overcast | Uniform |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MF:1 | 145 | -1.3e-14 | 2.4488 (-0.03%) | 3.1416 (+0.00%) |
| MF:2 | 577 | -2.0e-14 | 2.4493 (-0.01%) | 3.1416 (+0.00%) |

Exact: overcast 2.44954, uniform 3.14159.

### 1.3 Rectangular roof aperture, closed form

A point directly below the centre of an a×b opening in an opaque horizontal
plane at height h, under a uniform sky. The configuration factor from a
horizontal element to a rectangle sharing a corner above it is

```
F = (1/2π) [ X/√(1+X²)·atan(Y/√(1+X²)) + Y/√(1+Y²)·atan(X/√(1+Y²)) ]
DF = 100 · 4F        X = a/2h,  Y = b/2h
```

| Opening | Height | Exact DF | Measured | Error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 × 2 m | 2 m | 23.946% | 23.947% | +0.01% |
| 4 × 3 m | 2.5 m | 37.119% | 37.114% | -0.01% |
| 1 × 1 m | 3 m | 3.411% | 3.415% | +0.12% |

## 3. Cross-engine agreement

Mean daylight factor from each engine over the same grid. Walls are 0.10 m and
the glazing sits at the inner face except in the last row, so the reveal effect
is isolated rather than mixed into every case.

Split-flux still reads **a few per cent above** the raytracer on side-lit
cases and below it on skylights, so that offset remains the baseline to judge
the shaded rows against rather than zero. It used to be larger. Two defects
that had been partly cancelling each other were corrected together:

- The internally reflected component computed `5(ρ_cw − ρ_fw)` where BRE has
  `5·ρ_cw`, putting the IRC about 10% low.
- The sky component used the Moon & Spencer distribution `(1 + 2 sin α)/3`
  while the raytracer used CIE type 1 — up to 25% brighter near the horizon,
  a few per cent darker high up, so it biased side-lit cases high and
  skylights low. Both engines now use type 1.

Fixing only the first would have widened every gap, since it removed one half
of the cancellation. Together they took the 0.6 m overhang case from +3% to
0% and the louvre bank from +19% to +12%.

Two further things in the split-flux engine were calibrated against these
cases and are worth knowing about:

- A blocked sky direction is credited to the externally reflected component at
  **half** the device reflectance, because roughly half of what a blade scatters
  heads back out to the sky. Crediting the full reflectance, as the method does
  for a ground obstruction, put a louvre bank 83% above the raytraced answer.
- The aperture integration refines from 8×8 to **16×16 samples whenever a
  shading device is present**, because a five-blade bank puts structure across
  the opening finer than an 8×8 grid resolves. It costs nothing unshaded.

A louvre bank remains the case where split-flux is least reliable. Use the
raytracer for anything with fine shading structure in it.

| Case | Raytraced | Split-flux | Split-flux vs raytraced | Split-flux time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small window, thin wall | 1.30% | 1.39% | +7% | 5 ms |
| Large window, thin wall | 6.58% | 6.83% | +4% | 3 ms |
| Bilateral | 5.25% | 5.55% | +6% | 4 ms |
| Deep plan 12 m | 3.34% | 3.57% | +7% | 8 ms |
| Dark finishes | 3.21% | 3.39% | +6% | 6 ms |
| Light finishes | 4.07% | 4.49% | +10% | 3 ms |
| 0.6 m overhang | 2.52% | 2.52% | +0% | 19 ms |

## 4. Known limits, measured

### 4.1 Direct-sun disc sampling

The annual loop takes **one** sample of the sun disc per hour, giving a binary
shadow with no penumbra. Compared against 16 samples over five hours of a clear
June day:

- 1 sample: 4596.2 lx mean direct
- 16 samples: 4646.9 lx mean direct
- **Difference: -1.09%**

This is the accuracy floor for ASE and Direct sun hours near a shadow edge.

### 4.2 Bounce limit in very bright rooms

Paths terminate on the bounce limit, on Russian roulette below 2% of the
starting weight, or on a hard cutoff at 0.1% of it — both thresholds relative
to `π/N`, so they behave the same at every ray count (see §2.1).

The bounce **limit** is the one that bites. In a deliberately bright room
(walls and ceiling ρ = 0.9, floor 0.7):

| Bounce limit | Mean DF | Gain |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 13.123% | — |
| 8 | 15.123% | +15.24% |
| 12 | 15.838% | +4.73% |

Going from 4 bounces to 12 adds **+21%**. The shipped tiers use 1–5 bounces, so a room with
very light surfaces is **underestimated** at the default settings. Use the Fine
tier, or read the result as a lower bound, when every surface is above ρ ≈ 0.8.
Ordinary rooms (ρ ≈ 0.5 walls) converge by 3 bounces — see §2.3.

### 4.3 Luminous efficacy

A simplified fit is used rather than the full Perez efficacy model, converting
the W/m² an EPW carries into lm/W.

| Solar altitude | Beam (lm/W) | Diffuse (lm/W) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 10° | 75.2 | 107.2 |
| 30° | 91.0 | 107.2 |
| 50° | 99.2 | 107.2 |
| 70° | 103.6 | 107.2 |

### 4.4 Not modelled at all

- Specular or directional glazing — transmittance is diffuse-equivalent.
- External context beyond a single obstruction angle (split-flux only).
- Blind operation schedules, so sDA and ASE are indicative, not IES LM-83.
- Spectral and colour effects.
- A flat 365-day year; 29 February in an EPW is skipped.
